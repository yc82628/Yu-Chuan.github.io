From the Kitchen to the Keyboard

I spent 2 years as a chef before I started writing code. In a kitchen you learn to work under pressure: everything in its place before service, no wasted motion, and a plate that's right the first time — because once it leaves the pass, there's no second chance.

That discipline didn't go anywhere when I changed careers. I just pointed it at a different craft. These days the mise en place is a clean project structure, and the rush is a deadline instead of a dinner service. I used to serve tables their meals; now I serve APIs their requests.

I've graduated in Bachelor's in Comp Science at BSBI / teaching myself and building projects], working mostly with [Python and SQL]. I'm looking for a graduate role / an internship] where I can keep learning from people who are better than me and ship things that actually get used.

How the kitchen shows up in my code

Mise en place. Set up before you start. Read the docs, sketch the structure, know where things go — then write. Debugging a mess you made in a hurry costs more time than planning ever does.

Check it before it leaves the pass. Nothing ships without being looked over. It's the same instinct behind testing your own work before someone else finds the problem.

Service doesn't stop. Kitchens teach you to stay calm when three things break at once and keep working the problem instead of panicking about it.
